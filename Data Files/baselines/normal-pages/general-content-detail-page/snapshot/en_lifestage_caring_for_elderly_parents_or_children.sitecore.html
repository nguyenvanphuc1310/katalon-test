Protection

Beyond your basic coverage, additional coverage ensures comprehensive care, creating a reliable safety net for you and your loved ones. This way, you can rest easy knowing they are well-protected against unexpected medical costs.
What you already have1

If you are a Singaporean or PR, you’re automatically covered by three national schemes:

  1. MediSave
    A national savings scheme which helps CPF members save for future medical expenses, especially during retirement.
  2. MediShield Life
    A health insurance for large hospital bills and selected outpatient treatment costs.
  3. CareShield Life
    Long-term care insurance scheme for financial support and monthly cash payouts in event of severe disability.
What you may consider2,4

Are you fully prepared for life's surprises with your current protection policies? It's crucial to ensure you have enough coverage to avoid a protection gap – when your savings, investments, and insurance fall short of covering unexpected expenses due to health issues.

Here's how to find out your protection gap3:

  1. Calculate Needs: Multiply your annual income by nine (for death) or four (for critical illness)2. This is what your family will need to manage without your income.
  2. Assess Resources: Add up your savings, assets, and current coverage. These are the funds available to your family.
  3. Find The Gap: Subtract the needs from the resources.

If the result is negative, there's a protection gap. This means your family might struggle financially if something happens to you.


National schemes do not fully cover:

  1. Hospitalisation
    An Integrated Shield Plan (IP) provides additional coverage for private hospitals or higher ward classes in public hospitals.
  2. Critical Illness
    You’ll need coverage of 4x annual income. (Recommended)4
  3. Death & Total and Permanent Disability
    You’ll need coverage of 9 – 10x annual income. (Recommended)4

It’s recommended to spend at most 15% of take-home pay on insurance protection.

Why Act Now

Youth often means better health, allowing higher insurance coverage at lower premiums.
A sudden hospital visit can drain your savings, hindering financial progress.
Life's unpredictability can severely impact loved ones if you pass away prematurely.

Misconception: It’s okay not to disclose your existing medical condition.

Truth:
It's best to be open about your pre-existing medical conditions. Hiding them could result in claim rejections or even a terminated plan if discovered. Having pre-existing conditions doesn’t mean you can’t get insured in Singapore; it just means there might be some extra considerations for coverage and premiums.

Misconception: I am not eligible for insurance because I am too old or have a pre-existing condition.

Truth:
You can still purchase health and/or life insurance in Singapore, though some additional considerations will be factored in due to age or pre-existing conditions. There are specialised plans so it’s best to consult a Financial Representative to understand what’s best for your condition, age and financial goals!

Misconception: Having health insurance means all treatments are fully covered.

Truth:
Not always. Policies can have limits, gaps, and exclusions. It's a good idea to review your coverage, spot any gaps, and consider extra insurance or add-ons based on your health and family history to avoid unexpected medical bills.

Wealth

Building your future wealth becomes even more crucial when you have loved ones relying on you. Besides health protection, a proper wealth plan can empower you to achieve your long-term goals and leave a lasting legacy.
Basics – what you should have4

  1. Emergency Funds
    At least 3 – 6 months’ worth of expenses (including dependants) for rainy days.
  2. Legacy Planning
    A plan for a will, family trusts, life insurance, powers of attorney, and CPF nominations.
What you may consider

A retirement plan and savings for your upcoming milestones will give you peace of mind.


  1. Investment
    It is recommended to allocate at least 10%4 of your take-home pay for key milestones.
  2. A Retirement Plan4
    Sustain the lifestyle you desire with regular income to enjoy the best golden years.
    If you are 55 years old or above, explore options to monetise your property (Silver Housing Bonus, Lease Buyback Scheme, renting out room / flat).

Why Act Now

As lifespans are increasing5, you’ll need a plan for your financial needs in the future.
Give more time for compound interests to accumulate over the years6.
Supporting your dependants and lifestyle requires investing early in a diverse portfolio to let compound interest grow2,6.

Misconceptions? Let’s correct them.

Check out some common misunderstandings about financial planning and their truths.
ILPs are a risky investment move.
Investing is for the rich.
I will get better returns from investments other than life insurance.

Misconception: ILPs are a risky investment move.

Truth:
Unlike some other insurance plans, ILPs don’t necessarily guarantee returns, which may lead them to being perceived as risky. However, understanding your own financial situation and goals and carefully evaluating the ILP and its sub-funds can help give you more confidence to invest.
Yes, I want an expert’s help

Misconception: Investing is for the rich.

Truth:
There are plenty of investment options that don’t involve huge capital amounts. You may start small by investing some of your first few pay cheques in options that require lower capital amounts and are aligned with your needs. The key is to be consistent and think long-term!
Yes, I want an expert’s help

Misconception: I will get better returns from investments other than life insurance.

Truth:
It really depends on your life goals. It's hard to guarantee returns from investments or life insurance policies, but having life insurance provides extra benefits like financial stability for you and your loved ones. It also encourages regular saving, which can be tough to maintain through active investing. Since returns can vary based on market conditions and personal choices, it's important to match your financial goals with the right savings method. Talking to a Financial Representative can help you make the best choice for your needs.
Source: Savings & Investment, Life Insurance Association Singapore

For Your Dependants

Everyone gets peace of mind when the whole family is protected. Securing your loved ones’ wellness and ensuring coverage for the future benefits the whole family.
What they already have1

If they are Singaporean or PR, they’re automatically covered by four national schemes:


  1. MediSave
    A national savings scheme which helps CPF members save for future medical expenses, especially during retirement. You can also use your MediSave to pay for medical bills incurred by your immediate family members.
  2. MediShield Life
    A health insurance for large hospital bills and selected outpatient treatment costs.
  3. CareShield Life / ElderShield 
    Long-term care insurance scheme for financial support and monthly cash payouts in event of severe disability.
  4. Dependants’ Protection Scheme7
    Term life insurance scheme that provides basic financial protection for you and your family in the event of death, terminal illness or total permanent disability.
What they may consider

It’s important to plan ahead with proper and full coverage for your parents and/or children:


  1. Critical Illness
    Lump sum payout if they are diagnosed with a critical illness.
  2. Life Insurance
    Coverage for death, terminal illness and total permanent disability.
  3. Personal Accident
    Coverage for accidental death and injuries, including food poisoning, children-prone HFMD, and elderly-susceptible fractures.
  4. Integrated Shield Plan
    Additional coverage for private hospitals or higher ward classes in public hospitals.
  5. Savings/Investment Plans
    For milestones such as education and future home for your children and regular income during retirement for your parents.
  6. Legacy Planning
    A plan for a will, family trusts, life insurance, powers of attorney, and CPF nominations for your parents.
    If they are 55 years old or above, explore options to monetise their property (Silver Housing Bonus, Lease Buyback Scheme, renting out room / flat).

Why Act Now

Whole life policies can accumulate cash value and provide life-long protection at the same time.
Premiums increase with entry age for selected policies, your child can reap the benefits of lower premiums in the long run.

Misconceptions? Let’s correct them.

Check out some common misunderstandings about financial planning and their truths.
My life insurance coverage only needs to be twice my annual salary.
Only breadwinners need life insurance coverage.
The policy can only be in the name of the person who buys it.

Misconception: My life insurance coverage only needs to be twice my annual salary.

Truth:
In general, it’s recommended to have 9-10X your annual earnings as basic life coverage. However, this amount can vary depending on your family expenses and desired legacy, so it’s best to consult a Financial Representative who can give you a plan that fits your needs.
Source: 2022 protection gap study – Singapore, Life Insurance Association Singapore, 8 September 2023
Yes, I want an expert’s help

Misconception: Only breadwinners need life insurance coverage.

Truth:
Life insurance can be a great support for stay-at-home parents, helping to ease financial stress around childcare and home management. It also ensures your loved ones have a secure future, leaving a legacy for your dependants if anything happens to you or your partner.
Yes, I want an expert’s help

Misconception: The policy can only be in the name of the person who buys it.

Truth:
Life insurance policies can be assigned to another person, to transfer its rights from one person to another. As there are several documents and processes involved, it’s best to get in touch with us to smoothen the process if an assignment is desired! You can also buy life insurance to cover someone else, provided you have an insurable interest on the insured person. For example, parents buying life insurance to cover their children or spouses buying life insurance for each other.
